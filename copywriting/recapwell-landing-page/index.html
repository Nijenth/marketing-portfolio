01 · Brief

Who arrives here, and what they need to hear

Visitor
An agency owner or account manager who just clicked an ad about report week, saving time, or reports clients actually read.
Awareness
Problem-aware. They feel the reporting pain but may not know a tool can fix it this simply. The page has to connect the pain to the product fast.
Goal
Start a 14-day free trial, no card. One action only: no newsletter, no "book a demo" competing for the click.
Product facts used on the page
Connects GA4, Google Ads, Meta Ads, LinkedIn Ads and Search Console. Builds each client's monthly report automatically, adds a plain-English summary on top, sends it on schedule under the agency's brand. $89/month for up to 15 clients.
Constraints
New brand: no customers, logos or testimonials yet. Proof has to come from the product itself (show the report), the time maths, and a risk-free trial, not from invented quotes.

Questions agencies ask before trying
Can I edit a report before it goes out?

Yes. Every report waits for your review by default. Change the summary, hide a chart, add a note, then send. You can switch to fully automatic sending once you trust it.

Will my clients see the Recapwell name?

No. Reports use your logo and colours and are sent from your own email domain.

Which platforms does it connect to?

GA4, Google Ads, Meta Ads, LinkedIn Ads and Google Search Console. Each client's accounts are connected once.

Is my clients' data safe?

Recapwell only asks for read-only access. It can see performance numbers but can't change campaigns or settings.

What happens when the trial ends?

Nothing, unless you choose a plan. There's no card on file, so you're never charged by surprise.

03 · Rationale

Why each section is there

A
Message matchSingle CTA

The headline repeats the promise of the ad the visitor clicked, so they know instantly they're in the right place. The subheadline explains the mechanism in one sentence. The CTA says "my free trial" (first person, reward named), and the microcopy removes three objections: cost, card, and setup effort. Integration names act as early credibility for an unknown brand.

B
PASVoice of customer

Problem and agitation in the agency's own terms: five logins, unbillable hours, reports nobody reads. The "2–6 hours per client" range comes from the research and makes the cost concrete.

C
Reduce effort

Three steps make the solution feel easy. "Review and send" quietly answers the fear of losing control before anyone asks.

D
DifferentiationShow, don't tell

The plain-English summary is the one thing competitors don't lead with, so it gets its own section, and the page shows a sample report instead of describing one. Showing the product is the strongest proof a new brand has.

E
Benefits over features

Each feature is written as what changes for the agency: time back, reliability, their brand, one view.

F
Personalised value

The calculator lets visitors put in their own numbers. Seeing "43 hours back every month" for their agency is more persuasive than any claim, and it sets up the price as small by comparison.

G
Honest proofRisk reversal

No invented testimonials. Clearly marked slots show where beta quotes will go, and the trial itself becomes the proof: see a real report before paying.

H
Anchoring

One plan removes choice paralysis. Placed right after the calculator, the price line points back to the visitor's own number, so $89 is judged against the hours they just saw, not against other tools.

I
Objection handling

The five questions a cautious agency owner asks: control, branding, compatibility, data safety, and billing. Answering them right before the final CTA removes the last reasons to hesitate.

J
Closing CTA

Restates the core promise and the first small step ("connect your first client in about 10 minutes"), so the action feels easy rather than like a big commitment.
