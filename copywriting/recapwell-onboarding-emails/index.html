From free trial to paying customer

A 14-day onboarding sequence for Recapwell. Its job is to get each new trial user to one moment, seeing their first client report, and then make paying feel like the obvious next step.

01 · Brief

What the sequence has to do

Reader
An agency owner or account manager who just started a free trial, usually after an ad or a search. Busy, curious, and easily distracted by client work.
The activation moment
Seeing their first client's finished report, with the plain-English summary on top. Trial users who reach this moment understand the product; those who don't usually forget they signed up.
Business goal
Turn trials into paying customers at $89/month. The ad plan assumes 20% of trials convert; this sequence is how that number is earned.

Principles

  • Behaviour beats the calendar. Users who connect a client get a different email from users who don't.
  • One email, one job, one button. Every email asks for a single next step.
  • Written by a person. Sent from Sam, a (fictional) co-founder, in plain text, so it reads like a note and invites replies.
  • Honest endings. No card was taken, so the last emails explain exactly what happens when the trial ends: nothing, unless they choose a plan.

03 · The emails

Each email, and why it's written that way

Yellow fields are filled in automatically for each user. Subject-line lengths are counted live; around 45 characters or fewer keeps them readable on phones.

Hi {first_name},

Welcome to Recapwell. I'm Sam, one of the founders.

You don't need to set everything up today. There's just one thing worth doing: connect one client. Pick the one whose report you dread most.

It takes about 10 minutes, and then Recapwell builds their full report, summary and all, so you can see exactly what your clients would get.

Connect my first client

Sam
Co-founder, Recapwell

P.S. Your trial runs for 14 days and there's no card on file, so there's nothing to cancel.

Day 0 · on signupGoal: first client connected

Why it works

New users are most motivated in the first hour. Instead of a tour of every feature, the email shrinks the task to one client, and makes it personal with "the one whose report you dread most".

  • Subject promises the reward, not a "welcome"
  • Reply invitation builds trust and catches confused users
  • P.S. removes the "will I be charged?" worry early

Why it works

Users who don't activate in the first days rarely come back on their own. This email names the real blockers instead of repeating "don't forget to set up!".

  • "Totally normal" removes guilt, which makes people less likely to ignore it
  • Solves the most common blocker (access) inside the email
  • A human offer ("reply help") catches users a button won't
04

Teach the differentiator

S
Sam from RecapwellClients read three sentences. Make them count.A simple formula for report summaries clients actually read.
Day 4

Hi {first_name},

Most clients read the first few lines of a report and stop. So those lines carry the whole relationship.

Here's the formula Recapwell's summaries follow, and you can use it anywhere:

  1. What changed: the one number that matters most to this client
  2. Why: the cause, in plain words
  3. What's next: the action you're taking
Before: "Sessions +12%, CTR 3.4%, CPC down 8% MoM."
After: "Leads rose this month because the new landing page converts better. Next, we'll move more budget to the ads that feed it."
Edit your summaries

Sam

Day 4Goal: deepen value, build habit

Why it works

A useful email, not a sales email. It teaches a skill the reader keeps even if they never buy, which builds trust, and it happens to be exactly what the product does best.

  • Before/after example makes the idea instantly clear
  • Same theme as the content strategy, so the brand says one consistent thing

Answer the doubts

Day 11

Hi {first_name},

Before agencies switch, they usually ask me the same three things:

"Will my clients notice the change?"
Only that reports arrive on time and are easier to read. They carry your logo and come from your email address.

"What if a report says something wrong?"
Every report waits for your review by default. You can edit anything before it goes out.

"Is my clients' data safe?"
Recapwell only has read-only access. It can see results but can't change a single campaign.

See this month's reports

Got a different question? Hit reply.

Sam

Day 11Goal: remove reasons to say no

Why it works

Near the end of a trial, people stop thinking "is this useful?" and start thinking "is this risky?". This email answers the risk questions before the decision email arrives.

  • Questions are written in the customer's words, in quotes
  • Short answers: no feature lists, just reassurance

About {hours_saved} hours a month back, based on your numbers

To keep your reports building automatically, choose the plan: $89/month for up to 15 clients. Everything you've set up stays exactly as it is.

If you do nothing, your account simply pauses. There's no card on file, so you won't be charged, and your setup is saved for 30 days in case you come back.

Day 13Goal: convert to paid

Why it works

It opens with what the user has already built, so upgrading feels like keeping something, not buying something. People work harder to avoid losing what they have than to gain something new.

  • CTA says "keep", not "buy" or "upgrade"
  • Honest about what happens if they do nothing, which builds trust and avoids angry "you charged me" emails
  • An extension offer catches users who were busy, not uninterested
