Getting a new reporting tool found on Google

01 · Brief

The situation

Product (fictional)
Recapwell builds each client's monthly report automatically and opens it with a plain-English summary. $89/month for up to 15 clients, 14-day free trial.
Audience
Owners and account managers at agencies with 5–30 clients, searching for ways to make reporting faster or better.
Goal
Organic visitors who start a free trial, directly or after reading content.

The constraint that shapes everything

A new website has no authority with Google yet. Established tools and review sites already own the biggest searches like "client reporting software", and that won't change in 90 days.

So the strategy is: win specific, lower-competition searches first, build topical authority around one theme the competition under-serves, and let paid ads cover the big keywords in the meantime.

02 · Search landscape

What Google shows today

Before choosing keywords, look at who already ranks and what kind of pages win.

"Best client reporting software" → comparison lists

The results are almost all "best tools" listicles, and most are written by reporting-tool companies about their own category. Google expects a comparison page for this search, not a product page.

"How to write a client report" → guides and templates

How-to guides and downloadable templates dominate. Readers want something they can use today.

One theme repeats everywhere

Guide after guide gives the same advice: explain results in plain language, tie every number to the client's goals, and end with next steps. Plenty of advice, very few tools built around it.

What this means for Recapwell

  • Own the "explaining results" theme. It's what readers are told to do, and it's exactly what the plain-English summary does. Content and product tell the same story.
  • Publish useful templates. They match what searchers want and give a natural reason to try the tool.
  • Get onto the existing listicles, not just compete with them. Outreach to independent review sites can put Recapwell in front of buyers long before its own comparison page ranks.

Research: manual Google searches and a read of the top results, November 2026, including guides from Swydo, The Digital Project Manager, DashThis and Adriel. Search volumes and difficulty scores need a paid tool (Ahrefs, Semrush) or Google Keyword Planner, and are the first thing to validate on a real project.

04 · Topic clusters

Four clusters, linked together

Each cluster has one in-depth pillar page and shorter supporting articles that link to it. This shows Google that the site is a real authority on the topic, not a collection of one-off posts.

Cluster 1 · Differentiator

Explaining results to clients

  • How to report bad results to clients
  • How to write an executive summary for a marketing report

The theme every guide recommends and no tool owns. It's also the product's main feature, so readers meet the idea before they meet the tool.

05 · 90-day plan

What gets published, in what order

Bottom-of-funnel pages first (they convert even with little traffic), then the differentiator cluster to build authority, then templates to grow the email list.

Month 1

Pages that convert

  • Best client reporting tools for small agencies
  • White-label marketing reports (feature page)
  • 2 competitor alternative pages
  • Technical setup: site speed, sitemap, Search Console
Month 2

Own the differentiator

  • Pillar: How to explain marketing results to clients
  • How to write a monthly client report (brief below)
  • How to report bad results to clients
  • Outreach to 10 independent review sites and newsletters
Month 3

Templates & time-saving

  • Monthly marketing report template (lead magnet)
  • Pillar: How to automate client reporting
  • How to write an executive summary for a marketing report
  • Refresh month-1 pages using Search Console data

SEO compounds slowly: new pages on a new site often take several months to settle into their real ranking. That's why paid ads run alongside, and why month-1 pages are chosen for conversion, not traffic.

06 · Article brief

One article, planned in full

The brief a writer would receive. It connects a high-priority keyword to the product's main idea without turning the article into an advert.

Article brief · Cluster 1

How to Write a Monthly Client Report Your Clients Will Actually Read

Primary keyword
how to write a monthly client report
Secondary keywords
monthly marketing reportwhat to include in a client reportexecutive summary marketing report
Reader
Account manager at a small agency who writes 5–30 reports a month and suspects clients don't read them.
Search intent
A clear structure they can apply to next month's reports.
Angle
Lead with the story, not the dashboard. Most guides list sections to include. This one shows how to write the three sentences at the top that clients actually read.
Length
~1,800 words, with 2 before/after examples and a downloadable template.
Internal links
Pillar (explaining results), executive summary guide, report template, free-trial page.
  1. Why most client reports go unread

    Open with the familiar scene: hours of work, a client who skims page one and asks "so how are we doing?"

  2. Start with a three-sentence summary

    What changed, why, and what's next. Two before/after examples: a number-dump vs. a plain-English summary.

    Soft CTA: "Recapwell writes this summary for you"
  3. Choose metrics that match the client's goals

    Leads and cost per lead over impressions. A short table by service type (SEO, PPC, social).

  4. Explain the "why" behind each number

    Turn every figure into a cause and a decision. How to handle a bad month honestly.

  5. End with next steps the client can approve

    Close every report with 2–3 actions, so the report leads to a conversation, not silence.

  6. A template you can use this month

    The structure as a downloadable template.

    Lead magnet: template download → email → trial
  7. Make it faster: automate the data, keep the story

    Where tools help, and why the summary is the part worth protecting.

    Main CTA: start a 14-day free trial
