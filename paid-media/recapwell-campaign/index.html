01 · Brief

The product and the buyer

Recapwell (fictional)
Connects to GA4, Google Ads, Meta Ads, LinkedIn Ads and Search Console, builds each client's monthly report automatically, adds a three-sentence plain-English summary on top, and emails it on schedule under the agency's own brand.
Price
$89 / month for up to 15 clients. 14-day free trial, no card.
Buyer
Owners and account managers at marketing agencies with 2–20 staff and 5–30 retainer clients, in English-speaking markets.
Campaign goal
Free-trial signups at a cost that leaves room for profit (see the numbers below).

Why this is hard

  • Unknown brand in a crowded category. Agencies already know the big reporting tools. Ads can't win on "we have dashboards too".
  • Small audience. Agency decision-makers are a narrow group, so LinkedIn targeting is precise but expensive.
  • Low urgency. Reporting is painful once a month, then forgotten. Ads must catch people near "report week" or remind them of it.
02 · Research

What agencies say about reporting

The angles come from the problems agencies describe, not from the feature list.

Reports take roughly 2–6 hours per client, every month.

Estimates published by reporting-tool companies. Directional, since they sell the solution.

The first days of the month turn into "report week": exporting, copying numbers, fixing slides.

A theme repeated across agency blogs and vendor guides.

Data has to be pulled from many platforms and pasted together by hand.

The mechanical part, and the easiest part to automate.

Clients skim the report, or don't open it, and then ask "so how are we doing?" on a call.

The emotional part: the work feels wasted.

What this means for the ads

Most tools in this category lead with integrations and dashboards. That's what the product does, not what the agency feels.

Two feelings are under-used: dread of report week and frustration that clients don't read the reports. Recapwell's plain-English summary speaks directly to the second, so it becomes the differentiator.

Sources: published agency-reporting guides and case studies from AgencyAnalytics, Coupler.io and Metrics Watch. Next step on a real project: 5–10 interviews with agency owners and a read of r/PPC and r/agency threads.

03 · The numbers that set the limits

How much can a trial cost?

Before writing a single ad, work backwards from what a customer is worth. These are planning assumptions for a new product, to be replaced with real data after month one.

$89revenue per customer per monthPrice of the plan
~$1,600lifetime revenue per customerAssumes ~18 months average lifetime
≤ $400max cost to win a customerKeeps lifetime value at 4× acquisition cost
≤ $80max cost per free trialAssumes 20% of trials become paying customers

So every channel is judged on cost per trial (target $60, ceiling $80), not on clicks or impressions. If trial-to-paid turns out lower than 20%, the ceiling drops with it.

Google Search Capture

People already searching for a reporting tool. Highest intent, so it gets the largest share.

  • Exact and phrase match on category keywords
  • Competitor terms held back until week 3
  • Goal: cheapest trials, fastest learning

LinkedIn Reach the buyer

The only place to target agency owners and account managers by job title and company size.

  • Titles: owner, founder, account manager, head of client services
  • Industry: marketing & advertising, 2–50 employees
  • Goal: test which angle wins with decision-makers

Meta Cheap reach + reminders

Lower cost per impression. Best for retargeting site visitors and reaching freelancers and small agencies.

  • Retargeting: site visitors who didn't start a trial
  • Interest audiences around agency tools and PPC
  • Goal: bring visitors back during report week

Timing: spend is weighted toward the last days of each month and the first week of the next, when report week is happening and the pain is fresh.

05 · Message angles

Three reasons to care, tested against each other

Each angle is a different motivation. Testing angles (not button colours) tells us what this audience actually cares about.

A · Pain

Report week

"It's the 1st again. Say goodbye to the next two days."

Names the dread directly. Expected to work best with account managers who do the reports themselves.

B · Outcome

Time back

"Client reports that build and send themselves."

Sells the result. Expected to work best with owners thinking about team capacity and margin.

C · Difference

Reports clients read

"Your clients don't read dashboards. They read three sentences."

The one thing competitors don't lead with. Riskier, but it's the angle that can make an unknown brand memorable.

08 · Video script

30-second creator-style video for Meta and LinkedIn

Filmed on a phone by someone who looks like the buyer: an agency account manager at their desk. Three hooks are written for the first three seconds, because that's where most viewers decide to keep watching.

Hook 1 · Pain"If the first week of every month is 'report week' at your agency, this is for you."
Hook 2 · Curiosity"Your clients don't read your reports. Here's what they actually read."
Hook 3 · Contrast"Twenty client reports. I sent all of them before lunch."
TimeOn screenVoiceover
0–3sClose-up, talking to camera. Hook text on screen.One of the three hooks above.
3–9sFast cuts: CSV exports, copy-paste into slides, calendar showing the 1st–2nd blocked."Exporting from five platforms, pasting into slides, fixing the chart that broke. Every single month."
9–18sScreen recording: connect accounts, report builds itself, summary appears at the top."With Recapwell you connect your accounts once. Every report builds itself, with a three-line summary your client can actually understand."
18–25sReport arriving in a client's inbox with the agency's logo."It goes out on schedule, under your brand. Your client gets the story, not just the numbers."
25–30sBack to camera, smiling. Text: "14-day free trial · no card"."Try it free for 14 days. Skip report week this month."
09 · Test plan

What runs when, and how decisions are made

WeeksWhat's testedDecision rule
1–2The three angles on LinkedIn and Meta, same format, equal budget. Search runs on category keywords.After each angle spends ~2× the target cost per trial ($120) with no trial, pause it. Keep the angle with the lowest cost per trial.
3Three hooks of the video script, all using the winning angle. Retargeting switches on for site visitors.Keep the hook with the best 3-second view rate and cost per trial. View rate alone can reward curiosity that never converts.
4Shift budget toward the best channel and angle. Test competitor keywords on Search.Scale only where cost per trial stays under $80. Raise budgets about 20% at a time so results don't swing.
